About Queen City Crossroads

Queen City Crossroads is about Americans at the crossroads of history and the choices they make when they get there. We share real peoples’ explicit words as they navigate major historical events — like emancipation, world wars, school integration — and explore how they unfolded in one place: Charlotte, North Carolina. Through this work, our goal is to inspire our listeners to make courageous choices today that benefit the common good.

Curriculum & Archives

Instructional Materials & Historical Archives

The production team carefully investigates authentic local archival documents, historic newspapers, personal ledgers, and municipal records when scripting each episode. Each episode comes accompanied by a primary source packet for classroom instruction, community book clubs, and civic discussion.

Emancipation Proclamation Local Ledger Entry (1865)

Archival Metadata & Context

Repository: Mecklenburg County Historical Archives / Charlotte Public Records

Significance: Contains direct transcriptions of legal declarations and firsthand notices made following the conclusion of the Civil War. Analyzed in Episode 1 for explicit testimonies of formerly enslaved Carolinians building community institutions.

Classroom Discussion Hook: How do official legal documents contrast with personal letters written by residents living through the same summer?

Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Court Records (1969–1971)

Archival Metadata & Context

Repository: Federal District Court Records & University Archives

Significance: Verbatim hearing transcripts from the landmark Supreme Court ruling on desegregation busing. Shows parents, students, and community leaders debating the path toward educational equity.

Classroom Discussion Hook: What arguments were made for the common good versus individual preference during the 1970 public town halls?

Western Democrat & Local Press Clippings (1898)

Archival Metadata & Context

Repository: North Carolina Historic Newspaper Repository

Significance: Newsprint artifacts showing how local media framed voting rights, statewide campaigns, and racial cooperation in the late 19th century Piedmont.

Classroom Discussion Hook: How did editorial rhetoric influence citizen attitudes during pivotal election cycles?

Trade & Tryon Directory & Municipal Minutes (1910)

Archival Metadata & Context

Repository: Charlotte Public Library Robinson-Spangler Carolina Room

Significance: Business listings, merchant petitions, and transit routes centered around the historic crossroads of Trade and Tryon streets as modern electric streetcars reshaped neighborhood integration.

Classroom Discussion Hook: What physical boundaries and public investments dictated how Charlotte grew from a trading crossroads to a commercial hub?

Founder & Director

Sara Rich Merlo

Award-winning educator and entrepreneur. Founder and CEO of Merlo Education Consulting, she draws on her expertise in curriculum, academic innovation and high-stakes writing to help clients define their program models and build the systems needed to bring them to life.

Prior to consulting, Merlo was Vice President of School Design at NewGlobe Education, where she shaped much of the company's academic core, from distilling foundational teaching skills to leading a multinational team of instructional designers.

Merlo spent the first decade of her career teaching history and language arts in the U.S., Europe, and Africa. During her four years living in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, she founded a teacher coaching nonprofit. Her work has continued to center on how we learn, teach, and make meaning of history, and how a deeper understanding of the past can shape the way people engage with their communities in the present.

Born and raised in Alexandria and educated in Virginia’s public schools, Sara earned a Bachelor’s in History and a Master’s in Teaching from the University of Virginia. After many years of globetrotting, she is proud to call Charlotte, North Carolina home. Her curiosity about the Queen City’s past deepened when she began wrestling with local neighborhood names like Providence Plantation and noticed how much history many people simply didn't know. That experience reminded her of the power of historical knowledge to shape how communities understand and feel about the places they call home, inspiring her to create Queen City Crossroads: The Charlotte History Podcast.

Brandon Lunsford

Historian

Learn More Hide Bio

Brandon Lunsford has been the Historian at Mecklenburg County Parks and Recreation since 2025, and has focused on connecting communities with places and spaces around Charlotte. Before his arrival at the county, Brandon had been the University Archivist at the James B. Duke Memorial Library at Johnson C. Smith University in Charlotte since 2009.

He wrote Charlotte Then and Now for Anova Books and Thunder Bay Press as part of his thesis project in 2008 and completed revised editions in 2012 and 2015. He received his MLIS with a concentration in Archives from the University of North Carolina at Greensboro in 2019, an MA in Public History with a concentration in Historic Preservation from the University of North Carolina at Charlotte in 2009, and a BA in History from UNCC in 2001.

Michael A. Betts II is a Durham, North Carolina based experiential sound designer and social documentarian. An assistant professor of media production in the Nido R. His work would never be possible without his partner Carmen, their two children, Xaris (5) & Miguel (2), and their puppy Maya.

Helen Schwab

Writer

Learn More Hide Bio

Helen Schwab is a longtime Charlotte journalist. She retired as arts + culture editor from The Charlotte Observer after more than three decades in the newsroom, where she wrote, edited, led community outreach efforts, diversified freelance staff, spearheaded new sections, reviewed restaurants, and secured and administered six-figure grant funding. She has written for the Charlotte-Mecklenburg Remembrance Project (a collaboration with the Equal Justice Initiative), on whose steering committee she serves; consulted for the PBS documentary “A Binding Truth,” based on two series written by her husband, Gary Schwab; and is at work on an 1860s Charlotte history project.

As a consultant for Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools, she co-conceived and wrote a multimillion-dollar federal grant for writing instruction, and continues to support both beginning and seasoned storytellers. She holds a BS in journalism from Northwestern.
